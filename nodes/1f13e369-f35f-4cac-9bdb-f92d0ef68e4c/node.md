A node's content captures what is known so far. It is the working document: conclusions reached, decisions made, evidence gathered, open questions identified.

Content is not static. It evolves as you think. Early on it might be rough notes. Over time it becomes polished conclusions. When a node is resolved, the content is the final answer.

What belongs in content:
- Decisions and their rationale ("We chose Postgres because...")
- Findings from research or analysis
- Working notes that will be refined later
- Summaries of conversations or interviews
- Open questions that still need answers

What does not belong:
- Content that belongs in a different node (put it there and link)
- Volatile state that changes frequently (make it a child node instead)
- Redundant restatement of the scope

The content is often the most-read part of a node. Make it useful to someone encountering this node for the first time. Lead with the conclusion, then the reasoning.
