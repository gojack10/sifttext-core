Your brain is a dense mesh of interconnected ideas. Millions of years of evolution built it for breadth: associations, pattern recognition, gut feel, context that spans your entire life. But you cannot serialize that mesh cleanly. Ask someone to explain a complex topic twice and they will use different words each time, emphasize different parts, maybe drift. The mesh does not output deterministically.

A language model is the opposite. It is a powerful beam of focused reasoning. Give it a well-defined scope and it brings PhD-level depth. But it is one-dimensional: it reasons in one direction, one context window, one conversation. It cannot hold the full mesh of a complex problem any more than you can write it all down.

The tree bridges the gap.

You decompose your messy, interconnected problem into nodes. Each node is small enough that your brain can think about it clearly AND the language model can bring full attention to it. The tree structure (parent-child hierarchy) gives navigation. Links between nodes (cross-branch, cross-tree) capture the associations your mesh-brain notices.

The flow:
1. A language model interviews you and extracts pieces from your messy thinking
2. Each piece becomes a focused node with a clear scope
3. The language model applies its full beam to that one node
4. You verify the output matches what you actually think
5. The node persists, enriched with depth
6. The language model does not need to remember. The tree remembers.

Neither side is sufficient alone. Your brain has the breadth but cannot serialize it. The language model has the depth but cannot hold the full picture. The tree is the shared structure where both contribute what they are best at.
