Tree thinking is a way of working where you externalize complex problems into navigable structure and collaborate with language models through that structure. It is not a methodology you learn and apply. It is what happens naturally when you decompose a hard problem and let both human intuition and machine depth work on the pieces.

The core insight: a complex problem has more pieces than anyone can hold at once. Humans and language models both hit that wall. A tree is shared structure where both sides work from the same map. You decompose the problem into nodes. Each node is small enough to think about clearly. The tree holds the big picture. Links capture the dependencies your intuition notices. Language models traverse the structure at depth you cannot maintain alone.

This section explains the ideas behind tree thinking. Not how the features work (that is in How SiftText Works) but why this approach works at all.
