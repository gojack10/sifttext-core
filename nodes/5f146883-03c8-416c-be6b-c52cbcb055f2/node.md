Think of a model's context window as a piece of paper. It is limited, and it gets thrown out when you start the next chat. You can fit only so much on it, and once it fills, the model loses the thread.

Most AI memory products hand you post-it notes to stick on the paper. The system decides which notes to write and when to show them. The notes take up the space you needed for the actual work, and you cannot see what got chosen or why. Memory happens to you, behind glass.

SiftText is a binder. You save the whole page or only the part that matters. You name the sections. The model files new pages and fetches old ones under your gate: it proposes, you approve. When you need something, you name the neighborhood and the model opens the right drawer. Nothing gets saved or surfaced without you seeing it.

This is the plainer-language version of Brain, Tree, Beam. Same core idea, told through filing instead of mesh and beam. Reach for whichever lands for the person in front of you. The binder framing wins when someone asks how SiftText differs from automatic memory, because the post-it contrast makes the loss of control concrete.
