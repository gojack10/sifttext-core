You do not need to arrive with a plan. You do not need to know how to structure your problem. You do not even need to be coherent.

The language model's deepest capability is turning unstructured human output into clean structure. This is the bridge between your brain (messy, associative, handles complexity, cannot serialize cleanly) and the tree (organized, persistent, navigable, machine-readable).

All of these are valid inputs:
- A rambling voice dump about a problem you cannot get out of your head
- A stream of consciousness typed at 2am
- A confused description of something you half-understand
- "I have this idea but I do not know where to start"
- A pile of notes accumulated over weeks with no organization

A separately configured language model can help extract structure from these inputs: ask clarifying questions, identify separate concerns, and propose nodes with clear scopes. Verify that its proposal reflects what you meant before authorizing edits. Local Core stores and checks the resulting files; it supplies no model, interview service or transcription. You can also organize the same material yourself without an agent.

This matters because the friction of getting started is the biggest barrier to doing good thinking. If you had to organize your thoughts before you could use the tool, most complex problems would never get started. They would stay in your head, tangled and overwhelming.

SiftText inverts this. Start messy. The structure emerges from the conversation. The language model is the structurer. You are the thinker. The tree is where the two meet.

Speaking is even more powerful than typing for this. When you type, your hands are expensive, so you compress. You abbreviate. You leave things out. When you speak, your mouth is cheap. You elaborate, follow tangents, say the thing behind the thing. A language model listening to your voice gets closer to the actual shape of your thinking. More signal, less friction.
